# Worked Example — attempt, then self-review

**This is a synthetic illustration, not a real task.** The two websites below are
invented so the full loop can be shown end to end. On a real task every claim would come
from the two Chrome tabs, and inventing detail like this would be the most serious defect
a submission can carry.

It demonstrates the case the guide cares most about: **the two lenses disagree**, so the
overall answer has to visibly weigh a trade-off rather than inherit a winner.

---

## The user request

> Build a landing page for a local yoga studio called Still Point. It needs a class
> schedule, an instructor section, pricing tiers, and a contact form. Calm, earthy
> palette — sage green and warm sand — with generous whitespace and a serif headline font.

**Split into two columns:**

| Style asked for (→ aesthetics) | Substance asked for (→ functionality) |
|---|---|
| sage green + warm sand palette | landing page for a yoga studio |
| serif headline font | class schedule |
| generous whitespace | instructor section |
| calm, earthy feel | pricing tiers |
| | contact form |

---

## Inspection notes

**Website A — visual:** palette held across every section; serif headline over a single
sans-serif body; wide margins; consistent heading size and weight; sharp, evenly toned
photography.
**Website A — behavioral:** all four sections present. Schedule stuck on one day, day
tabs do nothing. Contact form accepts input, no response on submit. Two of three pricing
buttons dead. Instructor cards static.

**Website B — visual:** bright default blue accent against sand; heading sizes vary
between sections; opening headline pressed against its background photo; badge overlaps
the price on the middle pricing card; one instructor portrait stretched.
**Website B — behavioral:** all four sections present and responsive. Day tabs swap the
schedule. Instructor cards expand to a biography. Pricing tiers link to sign-up. Form
validates a missing email and confirms on send. Top nav anchors land correctly.

## The six fields as submitted

**1. Aesthetics — A is better**

**2. Aesthetics reason** *(124 words)*

> Website A is better because it holds the sage green and warm sand palette the request
> asked for across every section, pairs a serif headline with a single clean sans-serif
> for body text, and leaves generous margins around each block so the page never feels
> crowded. Section headings keep one consistent size and weight, and the photography is
> sharp and consistently toned. Website B drifts to a bright default blue as its accent
> color, which sits awkwardly against the sand background, and it varies heading sizes
> between sections while pressing its opening headline hard against the photograph behind
> it. On Website B a small badge on the middle pricing card overlaps the price beneath it,
> and one instructor portrait is stretched out of proportion.

**3. Functionality — B is better**

**4. Functionality reason** *(122 words)*

> Website B is better because every section the request named is present and responds.
> The day tabs above the schedule swap the listed classes, the instructor cards expand to
> show a short biography, each pricing tier links onward to a sign-up step, and the
> contact form flags a missing email address and returns a confirmation message once it
> is sent. The top navigation jumps to the correct section. Website A shows the same four
> sections, but its schedule is fixed to a single day and its day tabs never change the
> rows beneath them, the contact form on Website A accepts input yet does nothing when
> submitted, two of its three pricing buttons lead nowhere, and its instructor cards do
> not open.

**5. Overall — B is better**

**6. Overall reason** *(122 words)*

> Website B is better because this is a request a visitor has to act on, and only Website
> B lets them. Someone arriving to find a Tuesday evening class can change the day, read
> who teaches it, and send a message that is actually received. On Website A that same
> visitor meets a calmer, better-composed page, then reaches a schedule frozen on one day
> and a form that swallows their message without a word. The looser color and the cramped
> top of the page on Website B do cost the studio some poise, which is a real loss for a
> business selling calm. But presentation can be tidied in an afternoon, while a page that
> silently drops enquiries loses the booking outright.

## Self-review, run as the reviewer

**Pass 1 — mechanics.** Six fields filled. Word counts 124 / 122 / 122, all inside
40–160. Every candidate mention reads "Website A" or "Website B" in full; no bare
letters. Each reason opens with a verdict that matches its selected option. No sentence
or clause is shared between fields. Third person throughout, no mention of browser, tabs
or platform. **Pass.**

**Pass 2 — lens purity.** The aesthetics reason contains no control, feature, link, form
or loading language — an earlier draft said Website B "drifts to a default blue for its
buttons and links", which named controls inside the visual lens, and it was rewritten to
"as its accent color". The functionality reason contains no color, font, spacing or
layout language. None of the three bodies uses "aesthetics", "functionality", "visual
quality" or "usability" as a label. **Pass, after one fix.**

**Pass 3 — verdicts.** Each question was decided on its own evidence, and the three do
not all match, so inertia is not in play. No "Both" option was used, correctly: on
aesthetics Website A wins on palette fidelity, type consistency and spacing against four
named defects, and on functionality Website B answers four requested behaviors against
four dead ones — neither is arguable in the other direction. Website B was not rewarded
for feature count; both sites carry the same four sections, and the difference is whether
they respond. The overall reason weighs a specific trade-off — poise against lost
enquiries — rather than restating either field. **Pass.**

**Pass 4 — factual audit.** On a real task this is where each claim goes back against the
two tabs one at a time. Here the claims match the inspection notes, no failure is swapped
between the sites, and both websites are described in all three reasons.

**One judgment call worth recording.** The naming rule says the candidates must be
written in full "at every mention", but ordinary English needs possessives — "its
schedule", "its day tabs". Reading that rule to ban pronouns entirely would make the
prose unwritable, so it is read as banning *abbreviated names* — a bare "A", "B", "the
first one", "the other site". The mitigation is to keep pronouns close to the noun they
belong to and re-name the website whenever a sentence could be read either way, which is
why the aesthetics reason opens its last sentence with "On Website B" rather than "Its".

**Self-score: 5.** Three defensible options, three accurate reasons, each inside its own
lens and inside the word range, with the overall reason showing the weighing.

**What would have dropped it.** Leaving the "buttons and links" phrasing in the
aesthetics reason would have made it a **2** — a mixed lens is a major error even though
every word of it was true. Writing the overall reason as "Website B is better because its
day tabs work and Website A's form does nothing" would also have been a **2**: it repeats
the functionality argument and never weighs anything.

### Humanizer pass

The draft carried three real tells: a rule-of-three opening in the aesthetics reason
("holds... pairs... leaves"), uniformly long mid-length sentences throughout, and a
manufactured punchline closing the overall reason ("presentation can be tidied in an
afternoon, while a page that silently drops enquiries loses the booking outright").

Rewritten with the constraint brief attached — PERSONALITY AND SOUL suppressed, "Website A"
and "Website B" pinned as proper names, third person only:

**Aesthetics reason, humanized** *(127 words)*

> Website A is better because the sage green and warm sand the request asked for actually
> hold up across the whole page. Headlines are set in a serif, body text in one sans-serif,
> and nothing else creeps in. Margins are wide, so no section feels packed. Heading sizes
> stay put from one section to the next, and the photographs are sharp and toned to match.
> Website B goes a bright default blue for its accent, which fights the sand background
> rather than sitting on it, and heading sizes shift between sections. Its opening headline
> is pressed right up against the photograph behind it. On Website B a badge on the middle
> pricing card sits over the price underneath, and one instructor portrait is stretched out
> of shape.

**Functionality reason, humanized** *(121 words)*

> Website B is better because everything the request asked for is there and it all does
> something. The day tabs above the schedule swap out the classes. Instructor cards open to
> a short biography. Each pricing tier carries on to a sign-up step. The contact form catches
> a missing email address and confirms once the message goes. Top navigation lands on the
> right section every time. Website A has the same four sections, but the schedule sticks on
> one day no matter which tab is pressed. The contact form on Website A takes what is typed
> and then does nothing at all with it. Two of its three pricing buttons go nowhere, and the
> instructor cards on Website A never open.

**Overall reason, humanized** *(122 words)*

> Website B is better because a studio page like this one only earns its keep if a visitor
> can act on it, and Website A stops them. Someone hunting for a Tuesday evening class on
> Website B can switch the day, see who teaches it, and send a message that arrives. The
> same person on Website A gets a calmer, better put together page and then hits a schedule
> stuck on one day and a form that eats the message without saying so. Website B does pay
> for that in looks. The blue accent is off, and the top of the page is tight. For a
> business selling calm that is a real cost, just a smaller one than losing the enquiry.

Note what did **not** change: every factual claim, both verdicts, the lens boundaries, and
every "Website A" / "Website B" in full. The humanizer moved rhythm and diction only.

### Why the re-validation step exists

It was tested against simulated humanizer damage, and it caught every case:

| Damage a rewriter can plausibly cause | Caught as |
|---|---|
| "Website B drifts" → "the second site drifts" | BLOCK, abbreviated candidate name |
| an opinionated first-person aside added | BLOCK, first person |
| "is present and responds" → "is present, cleanly laid out, and responds" | BLOCK, visual language in the behavior lens |
| the overall reason recycled from the functionality reason | BLOCK, reused phrasing across fields |

The first of those is the realistic one. A humanizer told to reduce repetition will reach
for a synonym on the fifth "Website B", and that alone drops the task from a 5 to a 2.
